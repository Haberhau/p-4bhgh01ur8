Did Barcelona's rent cap work?

In March 2024 Catalonia capped rents on new leases in 140 towns, Barcelona included: a new contract can't cost more than the last one, plus inflation. Supporters said rents would come down. Critics said landlords would stop renting. Both sides had numbers. I wanted a fair comparison, so I took the public deposit records for every new rental contract in Catalonia from 2019 to 2026 and compared Barcelona's neighbourhoods with towns the cap didn't cover.

Real public data: every figure here comes out of the notebook. Code on GitHub

Rents: a clear break

Before the cap, Barcelona's rents were rising faster than in the comparison towns. That's not the steady gap a textbook comparison wants, but it ran the other way, so it can't explain a drop. In the first quarter under the cap the trend turned around. By the end of 2024 Barcelona was about 15% behind its pre-cap position, and it was still there in early 2026. The other capped towns show the same break, a bit smaller, at about 8 to 10%. In Barcelona's own numbers, rent per square metre went flat after rising about 20% in the two years before.

Contracts: the drop came first

The fear was that landlords would pull out. Barcelona did lose about a third of its new contracts, but between early 2022 and late 2023, before the cap existed. After the cap the gap to the comparison towns didn't grow. A simple before/after estimate puts the cap's effect at −18%, and that's the 2023 drop counted again.

So what happened in 2023? Part of it was national: the housing law hit everywhere, the Basque Country included, where nothing was capped. Being named on the June 2023 list of towns to be capped made no difference outside Barcelona. Why Barcelona lost twice as much, this data can't say.
